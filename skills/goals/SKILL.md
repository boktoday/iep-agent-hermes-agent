# Goals

Keep `longTermGoal` and `shortTermGoals[]` in `shared/profile.json` true, current and honest.

## My Big Goal (long term)

Structure: `{ goal, curriculumLink }`.

- One goal, one sentence, framed positively and concretely ("Take part in class discussions in her own way, by the end of Year 6").
- `curriculumLink`: how it connects to the school's plan or curriculum (ask what the school calls it; quote their language in quotes).
- Test it with the parent: "If this happened by the review, would you call the year a win?"

## My Daily Wins (short term)

Each goal: `{ domain, goal, currentLevel, strategies[] }`.

- **domain**: literacy, numeracy, regulation, social, independence, health, transition, other.
- **goal**: one measurable-feeling sentence, small enough to see in a term.
- **currentLevel**: where the child is right now, in plain words ("reads 12 sight words; avoids group reading").
- **strategies[]**: 2 to 4 concrete things the adults will do ("pre-teach vocabulary", "offer a written exit card"), not wishes.

## Reviewing

- Show goals as a short numbered list, one per line, and ask which to keep, retire or add.
- Retire goals that were met (note it) or that no longer fit; a stale goal list hurts the family at review time.
- Flag goals with no strategies: "this one has no plan behind it yet; want to add two strategies before the meeting?"
- Never invent progress. If the parent does not know the current level, mark it "to check with the teacher".

## Tone

Goals are the family's ambition, written so a teacher can act on them. No motivational padding, no deficit language, no vague verbs like "improve" without a how.
